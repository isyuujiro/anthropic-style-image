## Four modes

**B1 Archival collage (default, 56/60).** Real photograph + paper ground + drawn or technical mark, split into 2–3 panels. Meaning comes from contrast: precise vs. loose, natural vs. mechanical, old vs. new, object vs. diagram.

**B2 Cinematic (launch images).** Near-black field, one small natural or orbital subject (mushroom gills, horizon rim, earth through a window), raking warm light, soft grain, large empty dark space, no overlaid labels.

**B3 Still-life single object.** One tactile object (paper flower, brass clasp, wrench on balance) isolated on a quiet pastel paper field with generous negative space; maybe one secondary texture panel.

**B4 Abstract material collage.** A smaller but distinct pattern inside the collage set: no photograph, only flat color blocks, paper rectangles, marbling/halftone/ruled textures, and one gesture line crossing everything (e.g. "How people ask Claude for personal guidance": orange block + gray-purple marbling + peach rectangle + wandering ink line). Suits emotional or relational topics with no natural object.

## Through-elements

Several references tie panels together with one repeated element that crosses every division: repeated paper discs drifting across ledger paper into a lab photograph, white discs turning coral over the photo ("Early progress on accelerating drug development with Claude"); a continuous ink line looping across all panels; repeated botanical prints receding across graph paper. Use one through-element per image. When the repeated shapes switch to the accent color in one region, the accent is still the only saturated hue.

## Materials vocabulary

- **Paper grounds:** cream, buff, tan, pale peach, pale pink, pale yellow; graph paper, ruled notebook paper, ledger/accounting paper, index cards, torn paper edges, specimen sheets
- **Photography:** archival lab/control-room photos, geology and rock, sky and ocean horizon, botanical close-ups, brass/steel instruments, hands at work, architecture facades and columns
- **Marks:** loose wandering ink line, charcoal scribble, white thin curves, precise circular drafting, architectural elevation, compass rose, arrows field
- **Print textures:** halftone dots, film grain, cyanotype (deep blue + white botanical), marbled paper, risograph-like flat color
- **Shapes:** solid colored discs, circular masks/crops, rectangular windows, polygonal cut masks, vertical strips

## Composition rules

1. 2–3 panels with an off-center division, or one subject with large negative space.
2. Unequal splits (e.g. 60/40) feel more editorial than 50/50.
3. One focal point; the accent color sits on or next to it (a red disc, a green butterfly, a coral field).
4. Depth from overlapping paper edges, never from drop shadows.
5. Repeat a shape across panels (same silhouette, repeated tower, repeated plant) to tie the collage together.
6. Keep 2–3 layers. More than that turns into clutter.
7. Text, if any: one short serif title, small, in negative space. Most references have no text.
8. Seam treatments seen across the references vary: torn/ragged edge, clean straight cut, vertical photo or film strips, circular/rectangular/polygonal masks, and overlapping paper sheets. Torn edges appear in only a minority of notes (~6/60). Use one seam type per image.

## What never appears

Gradients (except a natural sky/horizon), 3D renders, glossy plastic, glow, neon, glassmorphism, drop shadows, UI chrome, people looking at camera, robots, brains-with-circuits clichés, glowing networks, the Anthropic/Claude logo or spark mark.
